2D intrusion cooling

A hot intrusion → heat spreads into the surrounding rock → water carries heat → the temperature pattern changes.

1. Choose the parameters

A 2,000 × 2,000 m domain. Water flows around an impermeable intrusion. The initial rock temperature and all thermal boundaries are fixed at 100°C.

A 40 m grid runs faster. A 200 m width requires a 20 m grid to place the intrusion boundaries on cell faces. Incompatible choices are automatically adjusted to 20 m.

Equations, assumptions, and model limitations

Steady flow: ∇·(K∇h) = 0, q = −K∇h. Left/right heads are prescribed. The top/bottom boundaries have no water flux. The intrusion is impermeable.

Heat transfer: C ∂T/∂t = ∇·(λ∇T) − ρw cp,w ∇·(qT). Conduction uses Crank–Nicolson; advection uses explicit upwinding. The two steps are applied sequentially.

Flow is fixed and does not respond to heating. Material properties remain constant. Latent heat, boiling, and buoyancy-driven convection are omitted. This is an idealized experiment. Material parameters follow Table 2 of Fu et al. (2010); geometry and boundary conditions are project choices. Full references are in the README.
